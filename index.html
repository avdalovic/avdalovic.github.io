Aleksandar Avdalović

Ph.D. Student in Computer Science and Engineering
Louisiana State University

I am a Ph.D. student in Computer Science and Engineering at Louisiana State University, advised by Prof. Elias Bou-Harb. My research is on trustworthy machine learning for cyber-physical security and safety. I study what an AI model must demonstrate before its output can guide a physical action, in industrial control systems (water, power, and chemical processes) and in vehicle networks.

Deep learning detectors that look strong on benchmarks can raise so many false alarms after deployment that operators stop trusting them. My work builds models that can be checked before they are trusted and stay reliable after deployment, using conformal prediction, topological and geometric deep learning, and formal verification.

Research

Before the output of an AI model guides a physical action, it should pass three checks. How much should we trust it, which part of the system does it concern, and can a person or a checker confirm why it is right? These three questions organize my work.

1. Uncertainty that survives deployment

Deep learning intrusion detectors for industrial control systems set their alarm threshold on historical benign data. When normal operation changes, that threshold fails. In our experiments, fixed thresholds raised alarms on 74 percent of benign observations despite a 1 percent target. I develop adaptive conformal calibration that brings false alarms back to about 2 percent without retraining the detector, graph-aware prediction intervals for industrial forecasting (GRACE-ICS), and calibrated intrusion detection for vehicle CAN networks, together with CANDLe, a reproducible benchmark that separates problems in the model from problems in its threshold.

2. Localization through system structure

An alarm should tell an operator where to look. I build detectors on the structure of the plant they protect, from factorization machines that capture interactions between sensors and actuators in water systems to HAID, a hierarchy-aware intrusion detector that encodes the Purdue model as a combinatorial complex using topological deep learning.

3. Verification of machine learning models

A model that guides a physical action should be checked against its specification before deployment. I translate decision tree ensembles into loop-free imperative programs, so that program verification tools can prove or refute safety properties of the model.
